# Entry 6
##### 6/3/26

### Final Presentation
For my final blog, it was time to present and give an elevator pitch on my freedom project, Dumpling Kitchen. Both were a little nerve wracking going in, but honestly came out of it feeling better than I expected, especially the elevator pitch. 

Here is my main source for this blog:
[My presentation slides - the deck I used for the in-class presentation.](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1SQoNnBdI9wv2o_eMtQW4nyswjagO3EIAW80wxakAI5w/edit?usp=drivesdk)


#### In-Class Presentation Takeaways
For the in-class presentation, I was pretty proud of how I walked through the Phaser concepts and explained the process behind building the game. Showing the GIF of the game running and pointing to the actual code felt like a strong part of the presentation it made the technical parts easier to follow.
That said, there are things I want to work on going forward. I think I could have made more consistent eye contact with the whole room instead of focusing on one side. I also want to keep working on projecting my voice and not just speaking louder, but having better breath control so it doesn't feel like I'm pushing to stay loud. The slides themselves I was happy with, the text was readable, the code snippets were clear, and I felt like I got through all the points I planned to hit.

#### Expo Elevator Pitch Takeaways
The elevator pitch actually went better than I thought it would. Going in I wasn't sure how it would land, but I ended up feeling really confident when the judges asked questions I knew my project well enough to answer them without hesitating, which felt good. I also think the hook about dumplings being 1,800 years old helped get people interested right away.
One thing I'd improve for next time is adding more details upfront about what makes Dumpling Kitchen different from just a regular quiz game or flashcard app. I felt like there was more I could have said about the cultural angle and the educational side of it, but I didn't get the chance to fit it all in. Overall though, I'm really satisfied with how the elevator pitch went definitely better than I expected and better than last year.

### Engineering Design Process
In my previous blog, I was on steps 5 and 6 of the Engineering Design Process (EDP) creating and testing a prototype. I'm now on step 7, which is to improve as needed. For me that means improving my presentation skills based on what I noticed during the in-class presentation and elevator pitch. Specifically, I want to work on eye contact and voice projection so I'm not relying on a microphone every time. Since this is my last blog, step 8 would be to communicate the results more broadly which I can work toward next year by continuing to present and refine how I talk about my projects.

### Skills
Below are the skills I've learned while working on this blog.

• Communication:
Presenting in front of the class showed me that public speaking is something you actually have to practice — knowing your project isn't the same as being able to explain it clearly under pressure. I want to keep working on voice projection and breath control, because when I try to speak louder for too long it starts to feel forced. Making more even eye contact with the whole room instead of one side is also something I'm going to be more conscious of next time.

• Consideration:
When putting the slides together, had to think about how they'd look from the back of the room. I made sure the font sizes were big enough to read from far away and simplified the code snippets so they'd actually fit on the slide without being tiny. If the text was too small or cramped, people sitting further back wouldn't be able to follow along so readability was something I had to actively think about, not just assume was fine.

• Time Management:
Going into this project planned for three full levels: easy, medium, and hard, with different recipes. I only finished one. The game works and it's complete, but I didn't leave myself enough time for the other two. Next time I'd
plan for one thing, get it done well, and only add more if time actually allows for it instead of building the full plan around finishing everything.

### Next Steps
For my next steps, I'd like to move on to step 8 of the EDP: communicating the results. I want to keep improving how I present, specifically getting more comfortable projecting my voice without a microphone, and making sure I'm engaging the whole room. I'm also looking forward to presenting at the SEP showcase and using everything I learned from this presentation to do even better there.
