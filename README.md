## Context
I am a student at [HSTAT](https://www.hstat.org/) in the [Software Engineering Program](https://hstatsep.github.io/). The "Freedom Project" for SEP11 is a year-long project all about making something using JavaScript along with a third-party JS tool.

For my project, I chose to independently study **Phaser** in order to help me make **Dumpling Game**.

## Implications
##### PROS
* Individual - Dumpling Kitchen gives players a low-pressure way to learn something cultural while having fun. It's a game, so the information sticks better than just reading a recipe off a page.
* Social - The game introduces players to Chinese food culture in an accessible way. It could spark curiosity about other cultures and cuisines, especially for people who didn't grow up eating dumplings.
* Education / Big Systems - Games like this could be used in classrooms or cultural education programs as a light, engaging way to teach about food heritage and cultural traditions.
* Finance - Dumpling Kitchen is completely free. Players don't need to buy anything or subscribe to anything to use it, which makes it accessible to everyone.

##### CONS
* Infrastructure - Running web based games requires servers and energy. At a small scale it's negligible, but if a project like this scaled up significantly it would contribute to carbon emissions from data centres.
* Culture - A simplified game version of a traditional recipe could be seen as reducing something culturally significant to a clicking exercise. There's a risk of oversimplifying the cultural context behind the food.
* Individual - Like any game, it could become a distraction. If someone is supposed to be doing something else and keeps clicking through the recipe for fun, that's time not being used productively.
